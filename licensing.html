Who it is for

If your platform caches what it approves, this gap is in your product.

Operating system and platform vendors

Mobile, desktop and embedded systems where a hardened or managed mode can switch on over caches filled before it.

Browser vendors

Cached WebAssembly, JavaScript and service workers that stay executable after the security policy changes.

Automotive OEMs and suppliers

Embedded vehicle platforms that hold cached executable content across a security policy change.

Enterprise security providers

MDM, endpoint protection and compliance tools that push a new policy to devices holding content approved under the old one.

AI and agent platforms

Cached agent skills, tool-access permissions and learned artifacts that were approved before a security policy change.

Cloud, IoT and embedded

Container and device fleets where cached content persists long after the policy that admitted it was replaced.

What is licensed

A method, not a product you have to adopt.

The framework is platform-neutral. It covers what happens to cached executable content at the moment a security policy changes, and you implement it inside your own stack.

01

Detect

The system recognizes that a security policy transition has happened.

02

Evaluate

Cached content is checked against the new policy, not the one it was cached under.

03

Mitigate

Content that no longer complies is dealt with before it can run, and the action is recorded in an audit trail.
